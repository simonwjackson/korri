# Diegetic summon — the fast index as an act, not a list

> Split from the blockbuster-ps1 ideation (idea #3). Full grounding context lives in the parent
> ideation doc: `work/items/parking-lot/01KVX589XXVGE3MM0G5GHY042Y-blockbuster-ps1-launcher-browsing-ux.md`.

**Description:** Give the mandated fast-find path without betraying no-A–Z/content-first: you express
an intent or trait ("horror," "something short," "unfinished," a known title) and the matching boxes
physically eject from shelves / a drawer slides out / the clerk bags three — results materialize as
*objects on a counter*, not rows of text. Power users who know what they want get a near-instant path;
browsers keep serendipity.

**Warrant:** `direct:` rough edges "no fast index/search" + no escape hatch for "I know exactly what I
want." `external:` Cover Flow's lesson — cover-first must pair with a fast jump. `reasoned:` pharmacy
"state a need → prepared handful" and seed-bank "request a trait → drawer pulls" both beat large-catalog
overwhelm by substituting authority-mediated retrieval for shelf-walking.

**Rationale:** The fast path is the documented requirement that keeps cover-first viable at scale;
expressing it diegetically keeps the identity intact.

**Downsides:** Needs trait/metadata tagging (leans toward a data model); "summon" animations risk
gimmickry if overused.

**Confidence:** 80%
**Complexity:** Medium
**Status:** Unexplored
